# Official Terminology Guide

**Version:** 1.0.1  
**Last Updated:** October 11, 2025  
**Purpose:** Ensure consistent, professional communication about Digital Service Design Schemas

---

## 🎯 Core Positioning

### What We Say

✅ **"Digital Service Design Schemas"**
- The official project name
- No qualifiers needed

✅ **"Comprehensive persona"**
- When emphasizing the 9-field depth and sophistication
- When contrasting with basic persona templates

✅ **"Professional standard"**
- When positioning the quality level and industry adoption goals
- When discussing validation and quality requirements

✅ **"9-field system"** or **"9-core-attribute system"**
- When describing the technical innovation
- When explaining what makes these schemas advanced

✅ **"Systematic barrier analysis"** or **"Barrier taxonomy"**
- When highlighting the most valuable innovation
- When discussing root cause analysis capabilities

---

## ❌ What We Don't Say

### Avoid These Terms

❌ **"Enhanced schemas"**
- **Why:** Implies there are "basic schemas" - creates false hierarchy
- **Instead:** "Digital Service Design Schemas" or "comprehensive schemas"

❌ **"Standard vs Enhanced"**
- **Why:** Creates false dichotomy - these ARE the standard
- **Instead:** "Traditional persona templates vs professional schema standard"

❌ **"Enhanced persona"**
- **Why:** This IS the standard persona format, not an enhancement
- **Instead:** "comprehensive persona" or just "persona" when context is clear

❌ **"Basic version"**
- **Why:** There is no "basic version" of our schema
- **Instead:** "Progressive implementation" or "phased adoption"

❌ **"Upgraded personas"**
- **Why:** Sounds like a software patch, not a professional standard
- **Instead:** "Migrated to professional schema standard"

## 📖 Preferred Usage Patterns

### When Introducing the Project

**Good Examples:**
> "Digital Service Design Schemas establish the professional standard for evidence-based service design practice."

> "Our comprehensive persona system captures 9 sophisticated behavioral attributes that traditional templates miss."

> "The barrier taxonomy is a systematic approach to identifying why problems persist, not just what problems exist."

**Avoid:**
> "Enhanced Digital Service Design Schemas offer an upgraded persona format." ❌

> "We have basic and enhanced versions of personas." ❌

---

### When Describing Features

**Good Examples:**
> "The 9-field system includes systematic barrier analysis, channel orchestration, and emotional moment mapping."

> "Comprehensive personas integrate with journey maps through barrier-to-friction mapping."

> "The professional validation framework ensures quality and completeness."

**Avoid:**
> "Enhanced personas have extra fields compared to standard personas." ❌

> "The enhanced barrier system improves on basic pain points." ❌

---

### When Discussing Quality Levels

**Good Examples:**
> "Validation scoring: Basic (40-60%), Professional (60-80%), Comprehensive (80-100%)"

> "Your persona meets the comprehensive quality standard with 95% completeness."

> "Progressive implementation allows teams to start simple and add sophistication over time."

**Avoid:**
> "This is an enhanced persona versus a standard persona." ❌

> "Upgrade your basic persona to the enhanced version." ❌

## 🔤 Specific Term Guidelines

### "Comprehensive" Usage

**When to use:**
✅ When emphasizing depth: "comprehensive behavioral analysis"
✅ When contrasting with traditional: "comprehensive vs traditional persona"
✅ When describing completeness: "comprehensive quality level (80-100%)"

**When NOT to use:**
❌ As a prefix to the project name: "Comprehensive Digital Service Design Schemas" ❌
❌ When it implies other versions exist: "comprehensive version of the schema" ❌

---

### "Standard" Usage

**When to use:**
✅ "professional standard" (quality positioning)
✅ "industry standard" (adoption goal)
✅ "standardized format" (consistency benefit)

**When NOT to use:**
❌ "standard vs enhanced" ❌
❌ "basic standard" ❌

---

### "Enhancement" Usage

**When to use:**
✅ "9-field enhancement system" (describing what the fields DO)
✅ "enhancement over traditional personas" (comparison to other approaches)
✅ "enhancement level scoring" (quality measurement - acceptable in validator)

**When NOT to use:**
❌ "Enhanced Digital Service Design Schemas" (project name) ❌
❌ "enhanced personas" (as if there are non-enhanced ones) ❌

## 🎯 Why This Terminology Matters

### Positioning Clarity
**These ARE the schemas** - not an "enhanced version" of something else. This is the professional standard we're establishing.

### Community Adoption
Professional terminology encourages serious adoption. "Enhanced" sounds experimental; "Professional standard" sounds established.

### Quality Perception
"Comprehensive" emphasizes depth and thoroughness. "Enhanced" implies comparison to a "basic" option that doesn't exist.

### Industry Recognition
Standards bodies and professional communities recognize "professional standards," not "enhanced versions."

## v2.0 Terminology (Actor / Mission / Experience)

These terms apply to v2.0 artifacts. v1.1 terminology (Persona, Role, Pairing, Journey) remains valid for v1.1 artifacts.

### Core primitives

| Term | Definition |
|------|-----------|
| **Actor** | A unified entity representing anyone who participates in a service. Replaces the Persona + Role + Pairing pipeline. Has three layers: Traits (enduring), Contexts (situational), Emergence (collision insights). |
| **Mission** | The persona-agnostic service graph — what the service does, how it's structured, what paths exist. One Mission supports many Experiences. Replaces the structural layer of a Journey. |
| **Experience** | What happens when a specific Actor goes through a specific Mission — their thoughts, emotions, needs, pains, and barriers at each step. The persona layer, separated from the service layer. Replaces the persona content in a Journey. |

### Key concepts

| Term | Definition |
|------|-----------|
| **Emergence** | Insights that arise from the *collision* of an Actor's enduring traits with a specific situational context. Not derivable from traits alone, nor from context alone — only visible when the two meet. Replaces Pairing synthesis. |
| **needAtStep** | What an Actor specifically needs at a particular touchpoint in a Mission — the micro-emergence that only exists when this person meets this moment. The highest-value field in an Experience. |
| **painAtStep** | How a specific step causes pain for this Actor — the gap between what they need and what the service provides at that moment. |
| **Lane** | A data layer declared on a Mission or Experience. Core lanes (channels, barriers, accessibility, emotion) have schema-validated structure. Extended lanes (frontstage, backstage, support systems) use simple types (text, list, metric). |
| **Node** | A single moment in the Mission graph. Can be a touchpoint, decision, handoff, wait, signal, start, or end. |
| **Edge** | A connection between nodes. Can be default (sequential), conditional (branching), error, timeout, escalation, or loop_back. |
| **Path** | A named route through the Mission graph. Missions have service-level paths (designed, observed, failure). Experiences have actor-specific paths. |
| **Phase** | An optional grouping of nodes for visual organisation. A rendering concern, not a structural constraint. |
| **micro-emergence** | The specific, step-level insight that only exists when a particular Actor with particular traits meets a particular touchpoint — captured in `needAtStep` and `painAtStep`. |
| **Provenance** | Where an artifact (or field) came from, and how confident we are. Tracks generation method, research sources, and AI tools used. Supports EU AI Act Article 50 transparency requirements. |
| **Governance** | Data classification, PII handling, anonymisation method, retention policy, and legal basis. Supports GDPR Digital Omnibus and EU AI Act compliance. |

### v1.1 → v2.0 term mapping

| v1.1 term | v2.0 equivalent | Notes |
|----------|----------------|-------|
| Persona | Actor traits | Merged into Actor |
| Role Card | Actor context | Merged into Actor |
| Pairing | Actor emergence | Merged into Actor |
| Journey | Mission + Experience | Split into service layer (Mission) and persona layer (Experience) |
| Journey step | Mission node + Experience node | Service content in Mission; persona content in Experience |
| Journey phase | Mission phase | Now a rendering grouping, not a structural constraint |
| Barrier (journey) | Mission barrier (structural) or Experience barrier (persona-specific) | Split by ownership |
| Moment that matters | Experience `momentThatMatters` | Now attached to Experience nodes |
